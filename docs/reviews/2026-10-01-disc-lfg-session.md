### Terms used below

- **Go:** a team pressing offensive cooldowns together, usually with crowd control on the enemy
  healer. Measured from the log: an offensive cooldown, plus any control and cooldowns chained to it
  within a few seconds.
- **A go "followed by a kill":** an enemy died during it or within a few seconds after it.
- **Locked out:** under a stun, silence, disorient or incapacitate, so unable to cast.
- **Peak:** the six seconds of a go with the most damage.
- **Their goes, "killed one of us":** one of our players died during or just after an enemy go.
  A death can be credited to more than one go, so this share runs high.

  played with.
- **Every measure of your own play is about the same in the 28–18 group and the 1–7 group:**
  - how much you were locked out;
  - how much you pressed;
  - how often you were idle;
  - when your Pain Suppression went;
  - whether your trinket was up when someone died.

  You didn't play worse today on any measure the log can take.
- **What changed was the team and the opposition.**

    three times the morning LFG games' 4.4. You queued at the MMR the fixed team had earned.

- **But "carried" is partly right, in a specific way.** Every game has a stretch where your
  externals are spent, and it looked the same on both days:
  - Both Pain Suppression charges gone about a minute in, pressed with a teammate genuinely in

  With your Pain Suppression spent, the fixed team's enemies killed one of you in 25% of their goes.

## Question 2: what should you have done

These are ranked by how strongly the data supports them. Today's sample is 8 games, so each one is a
lead to test, not a rule.

1. **Your Pain Suppressions and Medallions were needed. The problem is the stretch after both
   charges are gone, so play that stretch safe.**

   *(Revised after Chriso's objection: "if I didn't use it they would die or have to burn nearly all
   their major cooldowns." The first version of this file said to hold the second charge. The data
   below says he was right, and the advice was wrong.)*

   - **Were the presses needed?** Each was read with `warrant.php`. It takes the target's health and
     incoming damage at the press, then replays the window with the damage Pain Suppression removed
     added back.
     - In today's losses, 11 of your 13 defensive presses went on a teammate at 35% health or below,
       or with 5 seconds or less to live, inside an enemy go.
     - None was judged a waste, against 5 of 44 in the losses with the fixed team.
     - Your Medallions today mostly broke real CC during their go: Freezing Trap twice, Fear, Sleep
       Walk, Strangulate.
     - **Holding them would most likely have lost the teammate in that go instead of the next one.**
   - **What happened after:** a Pain Suppression charge is back about 3 minutes after it is spent,
     and both charges were gone about a minute into today's games. Enemy goes that started in that
     window:

     | | With the fixed team | LFG 30 Sep | LFG 1 Oct |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | Killed one of us, no Pain Suppression left | 25% | 67% | **86% (6 of 7)** |
     | Killed one of us, one or more charges left | 16% | 33% | 36% |
     | Both charges gone, first time (median) | 78s in | 61s in | 59s in |
     | Share of the game with no charge left | 38% | 39% | 52% |
     | Our goes a minute, no charge left / one or more | 1.1 / 1.5 | 0.9 / 1.3 | 1.3 / 1.5 |

     - **The fixed team could survive a go without your Pain Suppression; the LFG teams almost never
       did.** Our first death came with no Pain Suppression left in 6 of today's 7 losses, a median
       38 seconds after the second charge went.
     - **Today's teams also kept attacking at nearly the same rate with no charge left** (1.3 goes a
       minute, against 1.5). The fixed team slowed down more.
   - **What to do: treat "no Pain Suppression" as a phase of the game, and tell your team.**
     - When the second charge goes, say so ("no Pain Sup for a minute"), and play for time until a
       charge is back.
     - DPS stay near pillars and spend their own personals first.
     - Your CC goes on whoever is attacking, not on their healer.
     - Your team's next go is timed to start when a charge is back.

     That is what the fixed team did without being told, and what three strangers won't do unless
     someone says it.
   - **One press today really was spare.** At 15:44 your Pain Suppression went on the Arcane Mage
     0.6 seconds after he had Ice Blocked. He was immune, so it removed nothing and spent a charge.
     Watch for a teammate's own wall before pressing.
   - **One pair was late.** At 15:24 you trinketed a Polymorph and pressed Pain Suppression on the
     Rogue at 43% health. The burst was already over and the damage stopped on its own.
   - **Usable detail:** Pain Suppression can be cast while stunned. Against a Hunter's kill go
     (Intimidation on you, then Freezing Trap), the stun is the moment to press it, before the trap
     lands.
2. **Keep your Medallion for the CC that comes with their burst, which today it mostly was.** Your
   Medallions broke real lockouts during their goes, so this is not a habit to change. It is the
   reason the no-Pain-Suppression stretch is so dangerous: by then the trinket is usually gone too.
   At our first death it was available only 29% of the time.
3. **Press Power Infusion when a teammate's cooldown is visibly up, not on your own count.**
   - **What happened:** today 60% of your offensive cooldowns (mostly Power Infusion) had a teammate's
     cooldown within 6 seconds,
     against 74% with the fixed team. In 40% of them you were locked out within a second, against 22%.
   - **Why it matters:** with strangers you went in on your own read and walked into their CC.
4. **Say the plan before the gates open.** With LFG partners nobody had defined the go, and you felt
   it ("it felt like they didn't either"). Twenty seconds of typed or voice plan is enough:
   - who goes on whom;
   - what goes on their healer, and whose CC it is;
   - who calls trinkets.

   It doesn't fix skill, but it gives three strangers the same go.
5. **Expect the Hunter's trap, and stand for it.** Freezing Trap was on you in 7 of the 10 enemy goes
   that killed today. Where you stand when their go starts (out of the Hunter's line of sight, or
   behind a pillar from him) decides whether the trap chain lands. The log cannot measure position;
   this one is your call to check on video.

## Question 3: how to think about arena so you know when the go is

This is how the site's own arena model frames it (`data/brain/brain.md`, published at `/brain`).

### What actually decides a kill

**A kill is not decided by their health bar but by the list of buttons they still hold:** trinket,
personal defensives, the healer's externals, escapes and immunities. Track it per enemy player. Your
version as healer is the same list for *your* team: what does each teammate still have, and what do
you still have for them?

- **A go into a full list is a strip:** it costs them buttons. **A go into an empty list is a kill.**
  Plan them differently.
- The same works in reverse. When your second Pain Suppression is spent, every teammate's list is
  one shorter, and a good enemy team can see it (the buff is visible when it is used). That stretch
  is when your team should be hardest to kill, not busiest attacking.

### Knowing when the go is

**For your side.** A go is your teammates' offensive cooldowns plus CC on their healer. You can see
both on your frames: their buffs, and a Cyclone, Polymorph or Freezing Trap on the enemy healer.
- If both are there, the go is on: Power Infusion and your CC go in now.
- If only one is there, it's a strip, or nothing.

**For their side.** Their go is their offensive cooldowns appearing, usually as you get stunned or
disoriented.

**Your job in each:**
- **In our go:** hold your externals and keep yourself out of their CC.
- **In theirs:** spend for survival, and plan the trinket and the external together.

### Two defensives on one threat

When two of you answer the same threat (your Pain Suppression on top of a teammate's own wall), two
buttons are gone where one was needed. Until both are back, the team effectively can't play. In LFG
this happens because you see a health bar and they see their own cooldowns. The fix is a short
agreement: "I'll Pain Sup when you're under 50% and haven't walled" is a plan; reacting separately
isn't.

### Spending a long cooldown {#chains-caveat}

The model's own caveat: **a two-minute cooldown saved for a moment you never recognise is worth
nothing.** If you can't yet tell which go is the kill, using it early is better than holding it
forever. Your own games bear this out: your presses went on teammates with seconds to live, and
holding them would have lost those teammates sooner. So the lever is not *when* you press but what
the team does *after*: the stretch with no external is planned for, not hoped through.

### Counting time in goes, not seconds

Good players plan in goes and diminishing-return windows: "their kill go comes before ours", "we have
one more DR before they trinket". For you that becomes: **when will their next go be, and will I have
an external and a trinket for it?** If not, the go in front of you is the one to survive cheaply.

## What this cannot say

- **Small samples.** Eight 3v3 games today, 16 minutes of play. Every per-game pattern is a lead.
- **The log cannot see** positioning, line of sight, calls, or whom your team meant to kill.
- **Cooldowns are base values.** Pain Suppression is modelled as two charges, each back three
  minutes after it was spent. Talents can bring it back sooner, so "no charge left" is an upper
  bound on how long the stretch lasts.
- **"Would have died without it"** replays the damage Pain Suppression removed at the healing that
  actually happened. It cannot model how you would have healed differently.
- **"Defensive on the Mage in the last 30s"** counts curated defensive spells only. His own presses
  were checked separately against the raw log.
- **Experience is read from Blizzard profiles as they are now**, not as they were during the game.
  Highest rating is per character and Gladiator seasons are per account, so an alt shows a low
  rating beside many seasons.
- **Opponent strength is a confound.** Today's teams were the most experienced of the three groups.
  Some of the drop is who you met, not how you played.
