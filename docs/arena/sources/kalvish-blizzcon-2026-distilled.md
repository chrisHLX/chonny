# Source note — Calvish, BlizzCon 2026 AWC Grand Finals breakdown

**Who:** Calvish (Fire Mage / Sub Rogue), Gators Back. Won BlizzCon 2026 AWC as
Windwalker / Sub Rogue / Resto Druid, 4-2 over Echo.
**What:** ~90 minute post-tournament video. Preparation, comp choices, then a
game-by-game VOD review of the grand final with the replay in front of him.
**Distilled:** 2026-09-22. Raw transcript kept at
`kalvish-blizzcon-2026-awc-finals.md` — re-read it before distilling anything further,
the quotes are the value.
**Why this source is unusually good:** it is a post-hoc explanation of a plan that
*worked*, by the person who made it, against the recording. Not theory, not a coaching
video, not a highlight reel.

**Standing caveat.** He won. Everything here is contingent on the meta, patch and
opponents he won in. The player's own warning is the right frame:

> "What if there is a playstyle or talents that counter that idea — then he loses
> BlizzCon and we are reviewing someone else's blog."

Treat every claim below as *what a world-class team believed and acted on in this
patch*, not as a law of arena.

---

## 1. The answer pool is the running state [confirms Part 2]

The single most repeated idea in the VOD review. He narrates almost every kill by
listing what the target has left, never by health.

> "They don't have anything that can actually save them when they trinket right now."

> "You can have trinkets, but if you don't have anything to press, it's so easy to just
> die on the spot. Like at any point it's so easy to just die."

> "He has trinket, but he doesn't have anything to trinket into. So even if he does
> save W, he also can just die instantly."

> "W has trinket evasion, but it's hard to trade these in a way that will save both of
> you... we can kill WS through evasion if we have extended CC on JT."

**Target selection is re-derived every go from the pool**, and swapping mid-go on that
basis is routine:

> "Cub pre-roars him for the swap and we go JT. I instantly call that we have aggro
> trinkets... because I know he's dead here. He has no skin, he has no trinket, and he
> has no frenzy proc."

**The trinket is two-sided** — spent offensively to continue a kill:

> "We're literally intentionally calling aggro trinkets here... I get disarmed, I
> trinket the disarm, eviscerate him, and he dies."

> "I trinket aggressively here because I know that we have the damage to kill through."

## 2. Overlap ends the game [confirms Part 3]

> "We overlap iron bark, trinket, trinket, cloak. If you have ever played a game like
> this, you know exactly how unplayable the rest of the game is going to be. **You are
> not allowed to play until you have these buttons back.** So you will constantly be on
> the back foot until the game ends."

His own team repeats it in the final game, and he does not blame the teammate who
pressed second:

> "We end up overlapping trinket and cloak, which is obviously a mistake from us. I
> don't really blame Cub for trinketing because I am low HP and it seems like I'm going
> to die — but I have a lot of damage reduction up right now with faint and iron bark.
> I was very confident that I was not going to die at the point that I cloaked."

**Note the mechanism:** the overlap happened because one player could see his own
mitigation and the other could only see the health bar. It is an information problem
(§7), not a knowledge problem.

---

## 3. Damage buys your healer's globals and spends theirs [adds a third job — Part 4]

Not in the framework before this source. In the Rogue/Mage mirror he calls it the win
condition outright.

> "If you're keeping the consistent damage up and the pressure in this mirror, it
> enables your druid to go and CC the enemy druid... you want to be doing as much damage
> as possible to both the rogue and the mage, because if you're doing as much damage as
> possible you're building momentum for your druid over the enemy druid, to where your
> druid has a window to shut down the globals on the enemy druid."

The loss spiral, stated as a spiral:

> "If you're behind on healing as a druid, that means you can't go CC. And if you can't
> go CC, then you're losing even more pressure. It's the most snowball effect of all
> time."

And he explicitly warns *against* the setup-comp instinct in that matchup:

> "You don't want to be sheeping and you don't want to be trapped into doing these rogue
> goes. You want to just be doing as much damage as possible to both the rogue and the
> mage."

**Unresolved (open question B2):** what tells you which game you are in. He does not
say, and the player's counter is that "just do damage" can equally waste every damage
cooldown.

## 4. A go is engineered to deny globals [confirms Part 5]

The opener they won the tournament with was invented mid-series, between games, and
iterated twice. It is the clearest evidence in the source that **a written opener is a
discrete, installable improvement**.

Origin — a teammate asked a question after a loss:

> "Dazed turns to us and says: is there a way to make the opener better if I incap sweep
> the druid? And we start thinking about it and talking about it as a team."

The result is a **forcing sequence where every enemy reply is bad**:

> "We incap sweep the druid. Rogue comes out to peel. I gouge the rogue as he opens,
> because I'm still in stealth while he's soloing the druid. **And if the rogue sits in
> stealth, then Dazed is going to solo the druid and get a cooldown from the druid for
> free.** So the rogue *has* to come out... then Cub NS clones the druid, and then we go
> the rogue off the pre-CC gouge."

Second iteration, added a game later, scored purely in globals:

> "The mage blinks and then DBs us... so what we added is Cub roots the mage and then
> vortexes him. So he needs to blink twice to get out of the vortex and peel us with DB,
> **and that's two globals for him.**"

Effect:

> "This opener was literally cooked up on the spot from us... we do some variation of it
> every single game after the sewers game. So we just immediately levelled up."

**Simultaneity is explicit and load-bearing:**

> "I tell him to incap sweep here, and then I step bomb on the sweep global at the same
> time."

## 5. Time is counted in DRs and goes [confirms Part 7]

Seconds appear only when the number is decision-relevant. Otherwise the unit is the DR
window and the go.

> "We have one more DR before they have trinkets."

> "Their kill go will come before our kill go in terms of opportunities."

> "They're pretty much dead next DR no matter what."

> "We have combust kingsbane in 20 seconds."

## 6. Patience inside a window is never fully acquired [corrects Part 8 and Part 15]

The most useful correction the source makes. Holding a window where the enemy had
nothing, *saying the rule out loud on comms*, the reigning champion broke it:

> "We have 55 seconds to set this up. 55 seconds. And what's crazy, I'm the one that
> ends up rushing this go, and the entire time I'm saying in calls, I'm being super
> specific: 'Guys, we have 50 seconds to set this up, just do not rush it.' And I
> actually end up rushing it and completely throw this go... I think I was scared of the
> combustion. I don't really know. It's just a bad play. We had so much time to win
> there."

The opposite error, in the same series — over-holding:

> "Maybe I could have blinded earlier... if I did save blind for the next go, maybe we
> just win for free, because I can literally pre-blind."

**So there is no rule that resolves this.** There is only the answer pool and the clock.
And a diagnostic that reports a skill as *mastered* is claiming something this source
contradicts.

## 7. Information is manufactured, then broadcast [confirms and sharpens Part 10]

A repeatable routine, run every game, not an opportunistic read:

> "I intentionally eat CS a lot. Even though I know at this point that I don't want to be
> casting sheep, I'm still intentionally casting sheep to intentionally eat the CS a lot
> of the time, **because I can immediately call to Cubsy that the mage has no CS** — and I
> call this every single time I get CS'd and I tell him to cast clone."

> "Look at what Cub does. He roars next on his trinket and then half clones him. Things
> like this are happening every single game in every single mirror that we play."

**The broadcast is the mechanic, not a nicety.** Other examples:

> "I pick up the eyes. I target W and I ping him. So now my entire team knows he's behind
> this pillar." — followed immediately by a vortex and a rock from two teammates, and the
> sap that set up the winning go.

**The inverse — exploiting the opponent's model of your chain.** Cub: roar → begin
Cyclone → deliberately eat the Counterspell → shadowmeld/rake → Cyclone for real.

> "From Raichu's perspective this is a difficult position to play from. He assumes that
> since he started with roar, the only CC is roar. And if he CS's him, the go is over.
> So he instantly CS's and Cub fakes it."

## 8. Determinism is reached by recalling constants, not calculating [corrects Part 11]

A full Tier-1 kill computed live, under tournament pressure, in about two seconds —
out of four memorised facts, not a calculation:

> "W has evasion up at 20% HP. Touch of Death range is 15% HP, and Touch of Death is not
> dodgeable. What that means is if we get him into 15% health range, he will die to Touch
> of Death 100% of the time. So if we look at our tools — W is on stun DR, can't stun him
> and go through evasion. I could vanish shadowstrike him. But I also have blind, and
> **you can't dodge while you have evasion up**. So, while we're both out of range, blind
> is a ranged ability. I blind him. Dazed gets there, breaks the blind with blackout kick,
> gets him to 5% HP. Now the evasion doesn't do anything anymore. And now he Touch of
> Deaths him through the evasion and kills him."

The inputs were: ToD's threshold, ToD ignores dodge, Blind suppresses Evasion's dodge,
Blind breaks on damage. **This is the design instruction for the app** — ship memorisable
thresholds and interactions, not a calculator nobody has time to consult.

## 9. Sending into a landed answer [confirms Part 16's abort rule]

Two flavours, both present. Into a live defensive:

> "They combust deathmark kingsbane Cub who has barkskin. I think this is a massive
> mistake. I think this is never good actually. But they kept doing it multiple times and
> they never really got much from it... this makes the game way more unplayable for them."

Into the wrong target while another is bare:

> "We get cauterize block from the mage and iron bark from the druid basically on the
> first combust, and we still have death mark — but we ended up deathmark kingsbane
> bombing the rogue with kidney shot. And this is obviously bad **because the mage has
> nothing**."

## 10. Intent is chosen per matchup; dampening drives it [corrects Part 12]

His comp is a burst setup comp, and his plan against Mage/Lock was to not go at all:

> "Windwalker Rogue basically auto-wins into Mage Lock because of how dampening works. If
> they try to Mage Lock us, we just sit behind a pillar for 10 minutes and then kill the
> lock through every cooldown. **This patch, their cooldowns are affected by dampening**,
> making it very difficult for Mage Lock to beat Windwalker Rogue. It might actually be
> their worst matchup."

Against the comp that counters his:

> "Ret Warrior is the hardest counter to Windwalker Sub for sure, but we're playing
> 10-minute games... there's a lot of opportunities for them to just mess up and die. So
> it's not a complete free win, but it is a very very very long game."

**So the axis is not comp archetype. It is: which side does the clock favour, in this
matchup, this patch.** [Verification needed — open question C8 — whether dampening
scaling defensive cooldowns is live ladder behaviour or a tournament ruleset.]

## 11. Talents into matchups was a major share of the edge [confirms Part 13]

Prepared in advance, kept hidden, swapped per opponent.

> "I had a build that I kept hidden... you swap Critical Mass and Fires, and you swap to
> Sunfury Execution, and then you go and play Shimmer. This build is very good into
> basically everything they play."

A talent taken because of which *enemy spec* is present, not for throughput:

> "Since there's a demo lock in the game he's going to do basically the same damage
> anyways and you actually can leave him open. So I would have played Silhouette...
> Shadowstep's cooldown is reduced by 67% when cast on a friendly target. I would just
> have Dazed permanently running at Raichu and then I'm just stepping and following him
> around."

Whole-comp counters, measured:

> "Windwalker Monk was a 100% hard counter into Feral Elemental Shaman. We played 30 games
> of Windwalker Monk into Feral Elemental Shaman and had a 100% win rate. It didn't matter
> what we put in."

And the meta-point:

> "That's how experienced my team is in these types of matchups, and that's how confident
> we are in swapping our talents and builds around to make it good."

Also relevant: **comp selection is itself a prepared artifact.** The comp that won was
chosen months earlier ("we knew immediately as soon as the season started that we would
want a Resto Druid melee cleave"), from a written note of candidate cleaves.

## 12. Positioning decides rounds [confirms Part 14.1]

The project models none of this. It appears constantly.

- **Geometry denying a specific ability:** *"I evasion and I put my back to the wall so
  that I can't get kidney shot... which is why I think he didn't press it."*
- **Baiting a movement read:** *"I bait him to walk this way because he thinks I'm going
  this way to chase him. But instead I step to Cub, and using the movement speed from
  Shadow Step I turn the corner really fast and stun him."*
- **AoE placement deciding the round:** *"Look at how he bombs here. If he walked more
  this way and bombed, we are for sure dead."*
- **Pet LOS:** combusting from behind a pillar so the bird could not reach.
- **Cover as the whole plan:** the 10-minute pillar game vs Mage/Lock.
- **Covering a case with position, one global back:** Cub holds off JT *"in case the DB
  hits and I can't re-stun, and he's there to instantly maim him in case the DB doesn't
  hit."*

## 13. Three players, three views of the state [confirms Part 14.2]

Every winning play in the final involves information moving between seats fast:

> "I can immediately call to Cubsy that the mage has no CS."

> "I ping that he's behind this pillar. So now my entire team knows."

> "I instantly call that we have aggro trinkets. As soon as Cub roars JT I say: can we go
> JT? Go JT. We have aggro trinkets, just trinket aggro."

Also the source of the overlap in §2 — one player could see his own mitigation, the
other could only see a health bar.

## 14. Things this source does NOT support

Recorded so they don't get quoted as if it did.

- **Nothing about Solo Shuffle.** 100% coordinated tournament 3v3.
- **Nothing below Gladiator.** No account of what a 1700 player should do differently.
- **Nothing about mana attrition.** Tournament dampening ends games before mana does;
  the "healers run out of mana" win condition in the framework is unsupported here.
- **Nothing about ladder conditions** — no random partners, no queue meta, no comms via
  text.
- **Practice access, information asymmetry and comp-hiding** are a large part of the
  video and are almost entirely out of scope for a ladder product. The one transferable
  piece: *he was unable to practise against Mages because no NA team plays them* — which
  is exactly the gap a modelling tool exists to fill.
