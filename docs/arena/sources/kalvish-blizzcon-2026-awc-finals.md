# Calvish - BlizzCon 2026 AWC Grand Finals Breakdown

A comprehensive breakdown of preparation, strategy, and game analysis from the BlizzCon 2026 arena championship victory.

---

# Chapter 1: INTRO

Yo, what's up YouTube? It's Calvish here. My team just won BlizzCon 2026, the AWC Grand Finals, and I wanted to make a video similar to what I did last time for the North America Grand Finals where I go into depth on our plans. I wanted to make an even longer video going more in depth on our preparation as well as what we thought about the meta, the comps, other teams, and everything like that—basically a full, very in-depth rundown of everything this season that happened and how it was playing at Blizzcon.

The finals went to a 4-2 series, and we won as Windwalker Subrogue, which seems very random, but this pick was not random at all. We had a lot of preparation that went into this, and we knew exactly how good this comp was.

---

# Chapter 2: PREPARATION (PRE-BLIZZCON)

To start with our preparation and what we did leading up to the tournament, I have a notepad with a lot of information here. Basically, we don't have any mages we can fight on North America because the other NA team that qualified is F-Tier, and they only play Feral Elemental Shaman. EU has many very talented mages that were in this specific tournament such as Raichu, Maro, and P-Make. So immediately, we're at a pretty significant disadvantage when it comes to mage mirrors because our practice is difficult when we don't really have the same type of experience that Europe would in these matchups.

We knew this from the very start. Regardless, we had to do our best and practice the best we could. We had a couple workarounds that I'll get into later. But at the very start, we were fighting Feral Elemental from F-Tier, which is their only comp play. 

What we realized very quickly in the season is that an Orc melee cleave could have a lot of potential in the meta. We were specifically thinking about things like Retribution Warrior or Windwalker Subrogue or Retribution Windwalker or Retribution Survival. We were even experimenting with some Frost DK stuff after the frost decay buffs.

I want to specifically shout out Zang, who plays on Welcome to the Jung. Last season, Retribution Warrior was a very free matchup for Rogue Mage Druid (RMD), but Zang played it in a way that actually made it still winning for Rogue Mage, but made it so Rogue Mage could lose. He would do this by playing in a really unconventional way on a Druid where he would literally stand on top of his Rogue and his Warrior the entire game. That sounds like it would be terrible into Rogue Mage on paper, but Zang did it in a way that opened my mind and my teammates' minds on how you can play these weird Resto comps.

Even aside from Zang, we've had a lot of experience on Windwalker Subrogue in the past. We've always wanted to play Windwalker Sub, and Cub has a big strength as a Druid—he's going to be a lot better at maximizing the efficiency on these random druid comps compared to other druids. So we knew immediately as soon as the season started that we would want a Resto Druid melee cleave, and that would be ideal if we could play that as one of our strengths.

We were already practicing a lot of Rogue Mage, but we were also practicing some of those melee cleaves. Specifically, we would play Windwalker Sub and Retribution Paladin Windwalker the most. We had very high hopes for Windwalker, and I think Windwalker Monk kind of went under the radar a little bit.

---

# Chapter 3: ISSUES FINDING PRACTICE + TOURNAMENT REALM INFO

The admins took away the tournament realm from Welcome to the Jung, which was the team that played in the same AWC season. When I asked about getting them tournament realm so we could practice versus mage, they said "no non-Blizzcon competitors on tournament realm." This is acceptable.

But then, an hour after I hear this, I'm on the tournament realm and I randomly see Magnus just standing there on a character on the tournament realm. I don't really know what's going on with that, but it obviously made us feel extremely silly to say that literal competitors of the same season cannot have tournament realm, but somebody that doesn't even compete is allowed to just log on. I heard from other multiple people that they also have tournament realm, but competitors of the last season just get it taken away.

We tried to look for other alternatives to get practice into mages. There were basically two tournament realms—or actually three tournament accounts. You have your actual tournament account. If you had tournament realm, you could swap your server and log into the tournament realm. This means for us we get NA tournament realm because our main account is on North America. We only get access to the NA tournament realm.

Blizzard supplied us with Blizzcon accounts. Blizzcon is held in California in North America, so the Blizzcon accounts also connect to the NA tournament realm. But the European teams' normal accounts are on the EU tournament realm. This means the European teams had the opportunity to play on both tournament realms while we could only ever connect to one tournament realm at a time.

We ended up asking for European tournament realm access so we could hopefully practice versus the European players, and Blizzard did end up giving us the EU tournament realm, which is great. But this was the situation we were put into.

---

# Chapter 4: EU DIDN'T WANT TO WARGAME US ON NA (EGO?)

There was also some weird stuff happening. We shouldn't even really need the European tournament realm because logically, if you have to play on the Blizzcon account to play in Blizzcon, then the European teams should probably come to wargame us. The ping discrepancy is about the same region to region. When I play on the EU servers, I get like 140-150ms on a good day. When EU players play on the North American tournament realm, they get anywhere from 160 to 180ms. But on North America, we play with 50-60ms on our normal accounts. And on the Europe accounts, their normal ping is like 20-30ms. So they play with way lower average ping.

But the European teams did not want to wargame us. We literally had to go out of our way to intentionally set up characters that we would only use to wargame Europe, and we're playing on worse ping than them when they would have to inevitably set up the NA tournament realm anyway because you have to play on that account during Blizzcon. This was very strange to me.

I think a big reason is probably ego, to be honest. I think it's a very big ego thing. But my team does not care about any of this. We decided to set up on Europe and get some practice there even though they were very against setting up and playing on the North American tournament realms.
I believe Raichu was talking about how we did bad into them in wargames, but around 80-90% of the statistic he was pulling from was from the European tournament realm where we had 150ms, every single person on my team. That was a very strange situation to me. It really felt like an ego thing, but that's okay. My team is not afraid of losing. We don't see these games as relevant. And if the other teams want to care about the wins on such a disadvantage, that is completely fine with me.

We definitely lost a lot of games on the European tournament realm. A lot of this is due to ping, but it's also because we're not used to how these guys play, especially the mages. Fighting Raichu and Maro for me specifically was very hard to learn. Especially on high ping, we had a lot of trouble differentiating how good the information we were getting is because we're just playing on high ping. It's very hard to know if we're actually losing the matchup or if we're winning it or if we're getting outplayed or if it's just one of those situations.

But we did learn a lot from playing Echo specifically in the mirror. This ended up being very good for us that we had the opportunity to set up on Europe and play versus the EU teams. We still played versus F-Tier after getting Europe.

We also did something kind of funny. We would not have any mages, so in order to get Rogue Mage Druid practice (a very popular comp at this tournament), we had Whiz K play on a Mage, Meat Monk play on a Resto Druid, and Dallas play on an Assassin Rogue. So that was our practice for Rogue Mage games outside of the European games. As you can see, it was very very difficult to get some actual real information regarding these things.

# Chapter 5: WHAT WAS OUR PRACTICE AT LAN?

When we got to the actual tournament, we were mostly interested in wargaming Echo because we were pretty confident that Streamer Zone would be our opponent and that they would beat the Chinese team. The games we did get in the mirrors at first versus Echo went a lot better obviously because we're not on 150 ping anymore, but we still ended up losing the majority of them. I think we had 30-40%, maybe even less versus Echo at first.

But the day before the tournament, and I'll always remember this, me and Dazed were in our hotel room in the middle of the night talking about the mirror. We realized some changes that we could make that would make the mirror a lot easier for us. 

The way my team typically plays Rogue Mage comps and matchups is we play a lot around Counterspell and Stops, and cross-CCing the third target or the melee. What that looks like is me intentionally getting CS'd so Cub can Cyclone the DPS off of me, and then Cyclone Sheep the DPS, and we just do this constant rotation of CC on everyone on the enemy team.

The Europeans, at least Echo, don't do this. They just kind of do a lot of damage. We realized this literally in the middle of the night and adjusted our strategy completely for the next day. These changes made us go 5-1 in the mirror versus Echo on the tournament day on Sunday in wargames. We were feeling extremely confident in the mirror—those changes really helped us a lot.  
Those changes were the most noticeable into Streamer Zone. We also wargamed the China team a little bit—probably less than 10 games versus them. They were a lot better than I expected, specifically the Mage and the Paladin on that team.

**Practice Room Setup:**

I need to share something about the practice room and practicing at the tournament. Basically, we were all in one room right at the entrance. There were two entrances. The MDI players were on the top half of the room, and the AWC was on the bottom half.

We had four PCs lined up because there's four players on each AWC team. My team was positioned here, F-Tier was here, Echo was here, Streamer Zone was here, Larars's team (Guild Bean) was here, and OneLoon Travel was here.

The way they separated us was not with walls, but with curtains. So there were just curtains in between all the PCs, which made all the spaces very cramped. This was the most awkward situation ever. When we wargamed F-Tier, I could literally hear every single word they were saying. I could hear whatever they were doing to go, and they could hear whatever we were doing to go. They could hear what defensives we had left, where we're trying to go. You could literally be just walking around and listening to the people practicing, and you could hear their calls and what comps they're playing. You could even peek into their curtain.

It was not safe at all to talk about anything in there. You would have to walk all the way outside the room if you wanted to keep anything hidden. We did still wargame F-Tier, but it was very difficult due to being right next to each other. Pretty fried setup, honestly.

# Chapter 6: ENEMY TEAMS AND THEIR COMPS

**F-Tier (Feral Elemental Shaman):**

F-Tier performed very well and should be very proud of how their games went. I really hope the European teams have realized that F-Tier should absolutely not be underestimated. There was a lot of disrespect last season toward F-Tier when Feral Elemental was even better than it is now, with people saying they would never lose to this comp in Europe. I hope they've realized these guys are really good at what they do.

We wargamed F-Tier a lot, and they had their Feral Elemental Shaman comp. We had multiple things we could do into this. We felt okay about RMD into it—we played a lot of games and would win most of them. But this is probably not what we would have played into F-Tier.

**Windwalker Monk was a 100% hard counter into Feral Elemental Shaman.** We played 30 games of Windwalker Monk into Feral Elemental Shaman and had a 100% win rate. It didn't matter what we put in—DK, Rogue, Mage, Frost Mage, Shadow Priest, or Ret Pal. Windwalker just completely ran this comp over. We were very confident into F-Tier and didn't want to leak this and screw them over.

In terms of how I think they would have done into the other teams, I think they definitely would have beat Streamer Zone, and I think they had really good odds into Echo—I think it's probably more likely they would have beat Echo than lost to them.

**Echo (Rogue Mage, Rogue Lock, Mage Lock, Pharaoh Lock):**

Echo had a few comps we saw: RMD, Rogue Lock, Mage Lock, and Pharaoh Lock. These guys are all super good, specifically Raichu and W. I'm not really surprised by them—I already knew these guys were completely insane before coming here.

What really surprised me was Mercy. I knew Mercy was a god on Destro Lock, and we don't have any super good Destro Locks on NA right now, but I was still very impressed by his Warlock. Mercy is insane. I wasn't super impressed by Mercy's Warrior though. After playing against Blizzo, I'll probably never be impressed by a Warrior again. I have an extremely high opinion of Blizzo.

**Guild Bean (Ret Warrior, Warrior Mage, Rat Hunter, Boomy Warrior, Elemental Hunter):**

Guild Bean had a lot of random comps. I didn't even know about their Rat Hunter until they played it. Blizzo is actually a god—he's a complete god. I was extremely impressed with Blizzo. Lontar Ret Paladin reminded me of Sidu Presoker in his prime, very very good at Ret Pally.

Maro impressed me on Boomy. I thought his alts like Boomy and Hunter would be worse, but he was actually really good at his alts. I wish I could fight his Mage more. And lastly, Zepi was really really good on Rat, actually a lot better than I thought he would be for it being an alt.

These guys are all very experienced. I was impressed by their comfort and strategy in terms of comp picks, when they should lock something, and what map they should pick.

**Streamer Zone (Rogue Mage Druid):**

Not much to say about these guys. They only had Rogue Mage Druid to my knowledge. I think they're a lot better than what they showed, but they're probably not used to fighting a team like my team with an unconventional playstyle.

**OneLoon Travel / Chinese Team (RM Paladin, RMP):**

They had RM Pally and RMP, but the Healer was a one-trick Paladin, so they only played Paladin. They were a lot better than I thought they would be. The Pally and the Mage played pretty good. I didn't expect them to beat Streamer Zone, but we did end up losing a couple games versus them in the practice area. Props to these guys.
