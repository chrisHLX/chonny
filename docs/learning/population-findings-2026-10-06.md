# Learning from every player in every game

*2026-10-06. Asked: can the app give macro tips, can its general tips get better, and can the
system learn from the games themselves rather than only from the player who uploads them. This file
is the research and what was built from it. The numbers are regenerated by `wow:population`; the
tables here are the run of 6 Oct (795 games).*

---

## The short version

- **Yes, it can learn from everyone.** A game has six players and the coach used to learn from one.
  Measured with the same code as a player's own games, the archive holds **795 games, 4,263
  player-rounds, 1,193 different players and 33 specs**. That is now a committed set of norms
  (`data/population/norms.json`) that every page compares against, rebuilt by one command.
- **Four of the basics hold up across everyone, under confound checks.** Their healer locked in your
  burst, crowd control on their healer through the go, your damage landing together with their
  healer locked, and going in when their defensives are already down. Each one kills more often, in
  every stage of a game where there are enough goes to say.
- **One basic, as we measured it, did not hold on its own.** Pressing the big cooldowns within a few
  seconds of each other predicts kills only in short goes; across all goes it washed out. Damage
  *landing* together (both players in the same six-second peak) is the version that holds.
- **Macro tips are possible, and so are positioning tips, from the log alone.** WoW writes every
  failed cast of the player who logs, with Blizzard's reason. Out of range, not in line of sight,
  not facing, cast while moving, the Medallion pressed while down: 68,000 of them in your logs.
  Crowd control and kicks cast on someone other than the player's damage target are what focus and
  arena macros are for, and the log shows how often that comes up.
- **Everything is a correlation, and every line on the page says so.** A healer locked more is also
  a healer losing. The tables say what goes with winning; they are evidence for a tip, not proof.

## 1. The data

| | |
|---|---|
| Games | 795 (436 Solo Shuffle rounds, 270 3v3, 89 2v2) |
| Logged by | Crawlordx 386, Skylake 255, Dijonhoney 99, Env 33, and a handful of others |
| Players | 1,193 distinct (hashed; no names leave the machine) |
| Player-rounds | 4,263 (alive at least 45s) |
| When | April–May 2026 (season 1, 461 games) and August–October (season 2, patch 12.1, 334) |

**Two caveats that shaped the method.** The games are your games and your teammates', so the
"population" is the players at your ratings, not the whole ladder. And item level rises through an
expansion: with season 1's games in, the Beast Mastery median was 1.25M damage a free minute,
against 2.19M for season 2's games alone. So *output* norms
(damage, healing, presses) come from this season only (`SEASON_SINCE`), and every habit is split
against the median of the player's own spec *in their own season*.

## 2. What a go needs: kill rates across 3,975 goes

A go "kills" if someone dies in it or within 30 seconds. Both sides of every game.

| Condition | Kill rate with | without |
|---|---|---|
| Their healer locked (2s+) or kicked in the six seconds of peak damage | **32%** (1,196) | 25% (2,779) |
| Four or more pieces of crowd control on their healer in the go | **38%** (913) | 19% with none (613) |
| Damage landed together (both players 25%+ of the peak) with their healer locked | **34%** (749) | 23% with neither (1,211) |
| At least one of their defensives on cooldown as the go began | **30%** (2,888) | 20% (1,087) |
| Their healer kicked during the peak | 45% (93) | |

**Confound checks.** Later goes kill more (23% in the first minute, 32–33% after) and longer goes
kill more (21% at 10–20s, 37% at 45s+), and both could fake the effects above. Inside each band:

| | 0–60s | 60–120s | 120–180s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healer locked in the peak | 28% vs 21% | 40% vs 29% | 36% vs 32% |
| 4+ control on their healer | 37% vs 19% | 45% vs 30% | 33% vs 33% |
| Defensives down at the start | 31% vs 21% | 35% vs 18% | 34% vs 10% |
| Damage together, healer locked | 29% vs 22% | 43% vs 30% | 41% vs 31% |

By go length, healer locked: 25% vs 20% (10–20s), 29% vs 23% (20–30s), 38% vs 29% (30s+). The
effects hold where most goes are. Past three minutes the counts are small and noisy (dampening
changes the game), and the page does not claim anything there.

**What did not hold.** The gap between two players' *first presses*: within 3s killed 29%, apart
30%, because long goes (more presses, more time) dominate "apart". Inside short goes (15–25s) it
does hold: 27% within 3s against 21% apart. The Basics tab reads the damage overlap instead.

## 3. Habits that go with winning: 4,263 player-rounds

Each player-round against the median of its spec in its season; the win rate above the median
against below it.

| Habit | Better side | Win rate | Other side |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Healer: time crowd-controlled** | less than most | **62%** (732) | 39% (739) |
| DPS: time crowd-controlled | less | 55% | 48% |
| DPS: time not pressing anything | less | 54% | 48% |
| DPS: damage per free minute | more | 55% | 49% |
| DPS: kicks per free minute | more | 53% | 50% |
| Healer: crowd control per free minute | more | 54% | 47% |
| Healer: healing per free minute | **less** | 55% | 46% |

The last row is the warning label for all of them: a healer heals more on a losing team, because
there is more to heal. Healing output is not a skill read and the coach never uses it as one.

## 4. Positioning and macros: the logging player's failed casts

WoW writes `SPELL_CAST_FAILED` only for the player whose client writes the log. Over your 743 logged
rounds, after removing spam ("Not yet recovered": a key pressed before the spell is back):

| Reason (rounds with many against few) | Win rate with | without |
|---|---|---|
| Not in line of sight | **45%** (238) | 55% (505) |
| Cast interrupted | 46% (180) | 53% (563) |
| Medallion pressed while on cooldown | 30% (59) | 53% (684) |
| Cast while moving | 49% | 53% |
| Out of range, not facing, no target | no difference | |

The Medallion row is mostly a symptom: a trinket pressed while down is a round already going badly.
What the failures are best at is *naming the spell*: Skylake's lobby on 5 Oct had Pain Suppression
fail 11 times for line of sight, a teammate out of sight at the moment they needed saving.

**Macros.** Current guides (Skill-Capped, Patch 12.1, August 2026) still build arena play on the
same macros: focus and `[@arena1/2/3]` crowd control, party heals and dispels, ground spells at the
cursor, `/stopcasting` into a kick. Midnight's addon restrictions are about what addons may *read*
in combat ("secret values"), not about the combat log file or macro conditionals. What the log can
show is how often a macro would have been used: single-target control and kicks cast on someone
other than the player's damage target. Players who did that for most of their control won 54% of
rounds against 51%: a small lean towards controlling the healer while hitting someone else, which
is exactly what a macro makes one button. The log cannot show whether a player used a macro or
tab-targeted, so the tip is worded as "this came up N times", never "you did not use a macro".

Sources: [Skill-Capped, Feral Druid PvP macros, 12.1](https://www.skill-capped.com/wowarticles/guides/feral-druid-pvp-guide/macros/),
[Blizzard, Combat Philosophy and Addon Disarmament in Midnight](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24246290/combat-philosophy-and-addon-disarmament-in-midnight),
[Icy Veins, Combat addon restrictions eased](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/combat-addon-restrictions-eased-in-midnight/).

## 5. What was built

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| `RoundAnalysisService` **version 10**, `habits` | Per player: presses per ability, seconds free to act, control and kicks off the damage target, kicks given and casts kicked (by spell), a pet's hits on its owner's target; for the logger, failed casts by reason and spell |
| `wow:population` | Measures every archived game into `storage/app/population/` (hashed players, gitignored), then writes `data/population/norms.json`: per-spec norms (this season), the habit and go outcome tables, and the **evidence** table behind every tip |
| The **Basics** tab on every game (app and Your games) | Your team's goes (healer locked in the burst, control on their healer, defensives down, damage together), your control and casts (where it went, casts kicked, off-target control with a focus/arena macro for the spell you put on their healer), your output against every player of your spec this season (damage per free minute; for a healer, time controlled; buttons pressed well under the spec), positioning and macros from your failed casts |
| Improve | Two new habits tracked session by session: your casts kicked (against your spec), and casts blocked by line of sight (yours only) |
| Push to the website | The app now asks the server which measure version it stores, so a game is never marked sent at a version the server has not caught up to |

**Every number in a tip comes from the evidence table**, and a line with fewer than 20 rounds
behind either side states no number at all. When the archive grows, `wow:population` re-tests
every tip, and the wording follows the data.

## 6. How the system learns from now on

1. **Every game teaches about six players.** A new game of yours adds you and the five others to
   the population on the next `wow:population` run.
2. **The norms move with the season.** "Normal" damage is this season's, so a new season resets
   what "pressed less than most" means without anyone editing a number.
3. **The tips re-test themselves.** The evidence table is recomputed each run. A tip whose
   relationship fades shows a smaller gap, then no number at all; a new one is added by adding an
   entry to the evidence table and a line to `GameBasicsService`, tested against the archive first.
4. **Your corrections become rules.** Your Maim insight (melee control goes on the kill target
   because the melee is already there) became the range tie-break on the comp page the same day. A
   correction you make in a game note is the input; a rule with its evidence is the output; this
   file and `match-review-analysis.md` are where both are written down.
5. **An AI reads this file before it advises.** A model writing advice for a player starts from
   the evidence here, not from what it remembers about the game, the way the guides start from the
   Brain.

## 7. Open questions, next experiments

- **The first test of the coach on new players.** Chîll and Absolace: the basics check, then their
  games' Basics tabs, before and after reading the comp guide. The tables above are the yardstick.
- **Roots and Solar Beam's silence are not crowd control in the goes yet.** A Balance Druid's
  "root, beam" cannot show in the healer lock or the Basics tab until the timeline reads them.
- **Your own damage breaking your own crowd control** is not measured.
- **Per-spell positioning advice** (Pain Suppression out of sight is a team-positioning problem,
  Hammer of Justice out of range is a go started from too far) could be specific per spell.
- **The ladder beyond your games.** Every player here played at your ratings. A Gladiator's habits
  would need Gladiator games.
